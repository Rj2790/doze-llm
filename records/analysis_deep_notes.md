# Deep analysis — reading notes (2026-09-12)

Companion to `analysis_deep.md` (tables, generated by `eval/deep_analysis.py`)
and `analysis_final.md` (the preregistered verdicts). Everything here is
exploratory. Numbers are means over the five seeds unless stated.

## What the extra analyses add to the null

1. **The probe null is tight, not just "not significant".** Pooling every
   probe evaluation gives 3,900 item-evaluations per trained arm. Rates are
   0.32–0.34 with 95% intervals of about ±0.015; the conservative
   final-checkpoint pooling (300 independent items per arm) gives 0.33–0.34.
   Sleep-NoDream is slightly *below* chance (0.318, p = 0.04), which is what a
   guess distribution that mismatches the probe's answer mix looks like, not a
   shortcut. Upper bound on shortcut use in any arm: a few percent of items.

2. **Training taught execution, from the left.** Day-attempt accuracy in the
   trained arms went 0.46 → 0.89–0.92 across the twelve 50-episode blocks; the
   number of correct leading steps per attempt rose from 6.6 to ~10 of 11.
   Untrained errors cluster mid-chain (first wrong step mode 5–6); after
   training the few remaining errors sit later (mode 7–8). Working length did
   not shrink (97 → 97 tokens per attempt; median tokens per correct answer
   98 → 98): the model never abbreviated the chain, which is what a discovered
   shortcut would have produced. Only 3 of 978 held-out item-evaluations were
   unsolved by every trained arm at episode 600.

3. **Sleep lags Online mid-run, then catches up.** Block-6 day accuracy:
   Online 0.84, Sleep 0.70, NoDream 0.76; block 12: 0.89 / 0.89 / 0.92. Sleep
   only updates every 50 episodes, so it is always one night behind on the
   task; by the end the schedules are indistinguishable on the task
   (held-out last-3: Sleep 0.894, Online 0.889, NoDream 0.917).

4. **The batched schedule is smoother on the task and worse on the control,
   and the damage is early.** Held-out max drawdown: Online 0.18 (final 0.10
   below peak), Sleep 0.11, NoDream 0.09. On GSM8K, Online gained
   +0.013 per checkpoint, Sleep +0.003, NoDream +0.007. Splitting by time:
   Sleep's GSM8K change averaged **−0.013 per night over nights 1–4** and
   +0.010 over nights 5–12; Online's +0.033 then +0.003; NoDream's +0.007
   then +0.006. Early Sleep nights train 50 steps on a train set of ~36
   items (≈1.4 passes over very few examples, ~40% of them dreams), late
   nights on ~117 items (≈0.4 passes). Across all 60 Sleep nights the GSM8K
   change correlates −0.27 with night loss and +0.21/+0.22 with train-set
   size / new keeps: nights that are small and hard hurt the control most.
   So the H4 reversal is plausibly a data-poor-early-nights effect rather
   than something intrinsic to consolidation; NoDream, whose early nights
   contain only real keeps, does not show the early dip. This is a
   hypothesis from 5 seeds, not a result.

5. **Dreams were expensive filler.** Yield rose 0.14 → 0.32 across nights as
   the buffer (the dreamer's source material) grew; duplicates of the source
   puzzle stayed ~40% of proposals throughout; "wrong" rejections fell from
   26% to 11% as the solver improved. By night 12 dreams were ~40% of each
   night's new training items. Sleep vs NoDream: task −0.023, GSM8K −0.047,
   both n.s., for +80% generated tokens (≈45k dream tokens per seed). No
   dream ever touched a held-out or probe prefix (0 of ~3,660 proposals).

6. **Inference-time reasoning was inert.** Awake used 2 critique rounds on
   77% of attempts; accuracy by rounds used 0.40 / 0.41 / 0.42; day accuracy
   0.410 vs Baseline 0.405 at 1.9× the tokens.

7. **Buffers are clean and balanced.** Final replay buffers hold 336–454
   distinct puzzles (42–57% of the 801 training items, 46–61% of the
   training prefixes), answer mix within ±0.05 of the 1/3 each of the
   training set, zero items outside the training split, zero overlap with
   held-out or probe. The silent-failure watchlist item "buffer touching a
   held-out prefix" is confirmed clean from the artefacts themselves, not
   just from the code path.

8. **Consistency check passed.** Recomputing held-out accuracy from the
   saved per-item rows (model's parsed steps and answers) reproduces the
   harness's reported number exactly for all 22 runs that saved rows.

9. **Power.** H4 differences have mean −0.12 and SD 0.13; a paired design
   would need roughly 9 seeds for 80% power at that effect, and n = 5 cannot
   produce p < 0.0625 with the exact test.

## Implications for a follow-up (if any)

- A schedule comparison only makes sense on a task where the shortcut is
  discoverable at all; nothing in 15,000 trained episodes suggests a 4B
  model gets there with this task.
- If the sleep design is revisited, the early-night regime is the first
  thing to change: skip or shorten nights with tiny train sets, or scale
  steps to buffer size, and drop the dreamer unless yield and diversity
  improve (duplicate rejection alone consumed ~40% of proposals).
- Online's single-step-per-episode schedule is the better default for
  retention in this setting; Sleep's smoother task curve is real but did
  not buy anything on the endpoints.
